Brand Statement

Different Isn't Something to Hide.
It's Something to Embrace.

The Deaf Girl was born from Jesiira D’s personal journey as a Deaf individual navigating a hearing world. It is crafted with tender honesty to remind Deaf children, parents, and anyone who has ever felt out of place that being different does not make you less capable—it can make you limitless.

“When a child sees someone who communicates like them, walks like them, and lives like them on the front cover of a book, they realize their own story has value.”

— Jesiira D, Author & Creator

Courage

The bravery to stand tall and enter every room with your head held high, even when the world hasn't learned your language yet.

Identity

Embracing every facet of who you are—from your cochlear implants and sign language to your laughter and quiet passions.

Strength

Transforming what others call vulnerability into your greatest superpower and learning that resilience comes from within.

Representation

Giving Deaf youth a relatable, lovable hero on mainstream bookshelves who mirrors their daily realities and dreams.

Acceptance

Teaching hearing communities empathy, mutual respect, and how to embrace diverse ways of communicating with warmth.

Author Biography

Meet Jesiira D

Jesiira D is a Deaf author passionate about storytelling, representation, and Deaf awareness. Her journey inspired her to create The Deaf Girl—a story created to remind Deaf children and anyone who has ever felt different that they are not alone.

Growing up navigating spaces between the Deaf and hearing worlds, Jesiira experienced firsthand the longing for genuine representation in stories. She transforms her lived experiences with hearing devices, American Sign Language, and overcoming social hurdles into uplifting literature that touches hearts of all ages.

“Your difference can be your strength.”

— Jesiira D

Discover Sarah's World

“You don’t need hearing to have a voice.”

In The Deaf Girl, young Sarah navigates the tumultuous halls of school where whispers, unfamiliar sounds, and misunderstandings sometimes feel overwhelming. Using both American Sign Language and sleek purple cochlear implants, Sarah confronts playground bullying and the heavy desire to fit in.

With the support of true friends and a deep rediscovery of her inner courage, Sarah learns that she was never broken. Her journey is a love letter to self-acceptance and a testament to the strength that blooms when you embrace what makes you distinct.

The Origin & Purpose

Written for Little Jesiira

The journey from hiding differences to celebrating them.

“It’s okay to be different.”

As a young girl, Jesiira once felt ashamed of wearing hearing aids. She tucked her hair tightly over her ears and dreaded the curious questions that followed every conversation. But with time, she learned to embrace her Deafness and realized that what once felt like an obstacle was actually her most profound gift. She wrote this book for every child who still feels like hiding.

Advocacy & Community

More Than a Book.
A Mission.

Jesiira’s work extends far beyond literature. It is a dedication to spreading Deaf awareness, fostering inclusion, and honoring Deaf communities globally.

Representation

Creating stories that allow Deaf children to see themselves reflected positively, proudly, and as the courageous stars of their own stories.

Awareness

Helping hearing peers, educators, and communities understand Deaf experiences, communication styles, and the nuances of hearing devices.

Empowerment

Reminding young people that what makes them distinct can become their greatest asset in leadership, creativity, and resilience.

Education

Encouraging curiosity, dismantling myths about deafness, and teaching basic signs to build kinder and more inclusive schools.

Everyday Deaf Etiquette & Inclusivity

Simple, respectful practices to connect with Deaf and Hard-of-Hearing individuals.

Community Wisdom

Get Attention Respectfully

A gentle tap on the shoulder or a friendly wave in their line of sight is polite and standard practice.

Face the Person Directly

Maintain eye contact and ensure your face is well-lit. Avoid covering your mouth while speaking.

Learn Basic Signs

Learning even simple signs like "Hello", "Thank you", and "Friend" shows genuine care and inclusion.

Patience & Kindness

Never say "never mind" if someone asks for repetition. It diminishes communication and excludes them.
